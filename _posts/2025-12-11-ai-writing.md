I have the weird habit of sometimes opening Linkedin and looking at my own profile. I think it's probably some subconscious thing similar to what motivates us humans to look at ourselves in the mirror, searching for imperfections or just trying to keep an updated view of ourselves. Maybe it's because I look at my phone too much. I don't know.

Anyway, in one of those sporadic moments, I looked at my own profile text and remembered a conversation I had with my girlfriend this week about what a CV profile should say. Out of curiosity (and habit), I couldn't help thinking to myself: What would Karpathy do? What does Karpathy have in his Linkedin profile? So in a matter of seconds I typed "karpathy" in the search bar, but when I was about to click on his profile, I misclicked and searched the term instead, allowing Linkedin to show me the "closest" thing to Karpathy on the platform.

To my honest surprise, the top result was not Andrej Karpathy's profile (could it be because he doesn't post on Linkedin?), nor was it even a post that mentioned him. Instead, it was a post from an AI influencer. Following my curiosity, I opened his profile to see why he was the closest thing to Karpathy (I'm always on an almost "obsessive" mission to find interesting people and papers in the AI space).

Inside, I saw he had 1.5M followers (that could explain why Linkedin recommended him), and thus I wanted to see what kind of text a top voice wrote (especially now that I'm dedicating some time to writing myself). <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/pascalbornet_awsambassador-awsreinvent2025-sportsanalytics-activity-7405245122554077184-BuJJ?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAABa5Rg4B28_o_EZLw2KuMtrdpQKRKhPx0M4">Reading one of the posts</a>, I couldn't help but notice common patterns of AI writing:

* Using a question in the middle of the post to catch the reader's attention
* Phrases like "The more powerful the blablabla, the more human the blublublu..."
* Emojis or uncommon UTF-8 characters

Now, while my first intuition was that there is some AI involved there, I am not a native English speaker, nor am I an expert on the topic. I could be completely wrong, and in fact there is an interesting conversation to be had about how LLMs are influencing the way we speak. For instance, I have noticed that I am now using terms like "delve" and "showcase" more than before, which are indications of LLM usage, even when I am not using an LLM. 

Speaking of signs of AI writing, <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing">Wikipedia has an interesting article enumerating (with examples) different signs of AI writing, in case you are interested.</a>

That said, I don't believe that using LLMs means everything written or created will be slop. Nor do I think LLMs are "incapable" of creating creative or high-quality content (<a href="https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2025/02/25/what-is-interesting-writing-and-can-llms-create-it/">while researching the topic, I found this post by Jessica Hullman that I thought was interesting for reflecting on the matter</a>).

I think that Linkedin post struck such a strong chord with me (enough to motivate me to write this) because it resonated with the internal conversation I have been having about creativity, our "craft", and more broadly about "why we do what we do". In my case: Why build software? Why artificial intelligence? Why paint? Why spend a Sunday writing this post? And how do LLMs and AI in general fit into this picture?

Following that line of thought, I remembered the first webpage I made back in 2006 for the game <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metroid_Prime_Hunters">Metroid Prime Hunters</a>. A page built using <a href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_FrontPage">Microsoft FrontPage</a> that, if I recall correctly, was hosted by Telecable, my home's internet provider when I was a kid. I still remember the feeling of joy (which still accompanies me today) when I got it to work and shared the webpage with my group of friends.

Back then, there were no LLMs (nor even StackOverflow), so I remember reading through the book and dedicating part of my weekends to learning and building through trial and error. I remember searching for the "coolest" images and GIFs on the internet, <a href="https://www.trymstene.com/dancing-banana-gif-meme">like the legendary "Dancing Banana GIF"</a>: <img src="/assets/2025-12-11-reflections-ai-creativity/banana-dance.gif" alt="Dancing banana GIF" width="25" style="vertical-align: middle;">.

When I think about that webpage, the miniatures I paint, these blog posts, and even the software I write every day for my job, I realize that what motivates me is creating something that endures (even if just a little), something useful to others, or something "cool" that helps me express myself. and, if I can get the three of them together, that is a jackpot.

However, I don't think that "something" has to be made 100%

In that sense, I don't think it has to be something made 100% by hand. LLMs are just another tool for both work and expression. To draw an analogy (perhaps not a perfect one): it would be like saying something can only be considered truly human if it was made with bare hands, which is absurd. It would be like claiming that only the cave art of Altamira is truly human because they didn't even use brushes.

In my day-to-day, I use LLMs constantly for programming. Sometimes I write the code and ask them to review it, other times we work in tandem, and sometimes I let the LLM do everything while I review its work. Similarly, when I write text (especially in English), I like using them to discuss ideas and revise the text.

Como reflexion final, me parece interesante la analogia entre los LLMs o asistentes de IA en general y the Ring of Gyges. Creo que cuando los usamos a veces sentimos que su poder nos hace "invisibles", i.e., no se nota que has usado la IA, pero no es verdad, aunque seas invisible, si la gente sabe que tienes el anillo no les hace falta verte para saber que has estado ahi. Y por otro lado, me parece interesante como ese poder puede llegar a corrompernos profundamente, haciendonos olvidar hasta porque hacemos lo que hacemos.

Me vi el otro dia y cuando estaba escribiendo el post lo recorde, lo cierto es que me viene...como anillo al dedo.

Creo que es un tema muy extenso, soy consciente de que mucha gente lo ha tratado ya. Me he centrado principalmente es comentar LLMs pero hay una enorme discusion hoy en dia en aspectos como el diseño grafico y la musica, sobretodo ahora que los modelos se estan volviendo cada vez mas potentes. Vease por ejemplo el caso de Nano-Banana, mi modelo favorito 2025.

Tambien ser consciente del uso que hago de la IA en mis procesos de creacion, de tal forma que no acabe siendo "corrompido" por este enorme poder. Hay muchas maneras de utilizarlo pero si te despistas acabas perdiendo tu esencia y quien eres.

Por todo esto, me intento recordar constantemente porque hago lo que hago y la razon es porque me gusta, escrib posts porque me permiten organizar mis ideas, imprimo miniaturas y pinto porque mola, estudio y trabajo en el ambito de la IA porque me permite entenderme mejor a mi mismo como ser humano y soñar con crear seres inteligentes. En esta motivacion los LLMs son herramientas super utiles y estoy super agradecido de vivir en el momento actual porque lo que antiguamente era un sueño (Inteligencia artificial) poco a poco lo vamos volviendo realidad.
